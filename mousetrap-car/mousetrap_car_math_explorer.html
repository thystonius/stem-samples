String pull vs. car travel

The axle and wheel rotate together. For every centimeter of string unwound from the axle, the wheel rolls a longer distance based on the ratio of their diameters.

A larger wheel diameter increases the distance multiplier; a larger axle diameter decreases it. Bail length changes how much string is pulled, but not the wheel-to-axle multiplier.

Teaching model: This model now approximates string pulled using the straight-line displacement (chord) of the bail tip: s = 2L sin(θ/2). For a 180° swing, s = 2L. Actual string payout is the change in the string's length between the bail tip and axle, which depends on the mounting geometry; it is not necessarily equal to the bail-tip displacement. Actual car travel also depends on grip, friction, spring force and momentum. Here, “distance traveled” means ideal rolling distance without slipping, not a guaranteed experimental result.
